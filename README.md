# Women's Anaemia in Nigeria | Power BI

A Power BI dashboard showing which women are most affected by anaemia in Nigeria and where prevention can reach them, using the 2018 Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS).

## Project Overview
33,924 survey records were cleaned in Excel. Children's columns and women without anaemia were removed, leaving 4,415 women with mild, moderate or severe anaemia. Power BI was used to build the charts.

## Project Objectives
- Show which women are most affected by anaemia.
- Compare cases by age, wealth, education and place of residence.
- Look at iron supplement use.
- Suggest where prevention programs should focus.

## Tools Used
- Microsoft Excel (data cleaning)
- Microsoft Power BI (dashboard)
- Data visualisation and KPI reporting

## Key Insights
- **Severity:** Moderate 2,358 (53%), mild 1,931 (44%), severe 126 (3%). More than half are moderate or severe (56%).
- **Hemoglobin:** the average is 104.08, falling from 112.9 in mild cases to 67.6 in severe cases.
- **Age:** cases peak at ages 25 to 34 (about 51%). Only 4% are aged 15 to 19.
- **Wealth:** the poorest group has the most cases (1,089) and the richest the fewest (516).
- **Education:** 1,914 (43%) have no formal education.
- **Residence:** rural homes account for 2,924 cases (66%) and hold 90 of the 126 severe cases.
- **First birth:** 57% first gave birth between ages 12 and 19.
- **Iron supplements:** 81.02% reported No and 18.66% Yes. Use rises with wealth, from 10.8% in the poorest group to 28.5% in the richest.

## Recommendations
1. Target rural and low-income households with community-based programs.
2. Make iron supplements easier to get and explain them better, especially in the poorest households.
3. Reach women in their 20s and early 30s with nutrition and health education.
4. Use simple, spoken messages such as radio and community meetings.
5. Strengthen early screening in rural health centres, where 71% of severe cases are found.

## Limitations
The data covers women with anaemia only, so the findings show who the cases are, not which groups have higher rates.

## Data Source
2018 Nigeria Demographic and Health Survey (NDHS) from the DHS Program (https://dhsprogram.com). The data files are not included in this repository. They can be requested for free from the DHS Program website.

## Conclusion
Anaemia is not linked to one factor. The most cases are in rural, low-income homes with low iron use and women with little schooling. A combined approach of iron access, community health workers and simple communication is needed.
